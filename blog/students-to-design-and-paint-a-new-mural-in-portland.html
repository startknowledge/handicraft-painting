How Students Can Design and Paint a New Mural in Portland: A Complete Guide

Portland, Oregon, is a city where creativity thrives, and its streets are adorned with vibrant murals that tell stories of the community. From the iconic Portland Streetcar murals to the colorful Alberta Arts District walls, public art has become a defining feature of the city. But what if students wanted to contribute to this artistic landscape? Whether for a school project, a community initiative, or personal passion, designing and painting a mural in Portland can be a rewarding experience—both creatively and professionally.

This guide will walk you through every step of the process, from brainstorming ideas to executing the final strokes. We’ll cover practical strategies, common mistakes to avoid, and how to make your mural stand out in Portland’s competitive public art scene. By the end, you’ll have all the tools you need to turn your vision into a reality.

Why Portland Is the Perfect Place for a Student Mural Project

Portland has long been a hub for artistic expression, with a strong tradition of supporting public art. The city’s Portland Bureau of Development Services actively encourages murals as part of its Public Art Program, which has funded over 100 murals since 2010. Additionally, organizations like Portland Art Museum and First Thursday events foster a culture where street art is celebrated.

For students, this means:

  • Access to resources—Local art supply stores, community centers, and schools often provide materials or discounts.
  • Community support—Portland residents are generally open to new murals, especially those with educational or cultural significance.
  • Networking opportunities—Working on a mural can connect you with local artists, galleries, and future employers.
  • A portfolio boost—A well-executed mural can showcase your skills to colleges, art programs, or potential clients.

Whether you're part of a high school art class, a college club, or a community youth group, Portland offers the perfect backdrop for your mural project.

The Step-by-Step Process: From Idea to Finished Mural

Designing and painting a mural is a multi-phase project that requires planning, creativity, and teamwork. Below is a detailed breakdown of each step.

1. Define Your Vision and Purpose

Before picking up a brush, it’s essential to clarify why you’re creating the mural. Is it:

  • A school project with a specific theme (e.g., environmental awareness, local history)?
  • A community initiative to beautify a neighborhood?
  • A personal passion project to explore your artistic style?
  • A fundraising effort for a local cause?

Your purpose will shape every decision—from location to design. For example:

  • If it’s for a school, the mural might focus on Portland’s natural landscapes or historical figures like Chief Joseph or Emily Carr.
  • If it’s for a community space, you might collaborate with local residents to incorporate their stories.

2. Choose the Right Location

Portland has dozens of potential mural spots, but not all are equal. Consider:

  1. Visibility—Will pedestrians and drivers see it? High-traffic areas like Hawthorne Boulevard or Burnside Street get more attention.
  2. Surface type—Smooth concrete is ideal, but brick or wood may require extra prep work.
  3. Permits—Check with the City of Portland for mural permits. Some areas require approval from the Portland Bureau of Development Services.
  4. Accessibility—Can you safely reach the wall? Ladders, scaffolding, or even a crane may be needed.

Popular Portland mural locations include:

  • Alberta Street—A hotspot for murals, often featuring themes of music and culture.
  • SE 23rd Avenue—Home to the Portland Streetcar murals, which celebrate local history.
  • SE Division Street—A mix of commercial and residential areas with open walls.
  • Schools and community centers—Many offer walls for student projects (e.g., Portland Public Schools).

3. Gather Your Team and Materials

Mural projects work best with a team. Assign roles such as:

  • Lead artist—Oversees design and execution.
  • Project manager—Handles logistics, permits, and timelines.
  • Researcher—Gathers inspiration and local references.
  • Painters—Assist with brushwork and color mixing.

Essential supplies include:

  • Painting tools—High-quality brushes, rollers, spray paint (if allowed), and stencils.
  • Surface prep—Sandpaper, primer, and sealant to ensure longevity.
  • Safety gear—Gloves, masks, and non-slip ladders.
  • Design tools—Sketchbooks, digital tablets (for preliminary designs), and tracing paper.

Where to buy in Portland:

  • Blick Art Materials (multiple locations) – Affordable brushes and paints.
  • Portland Paint & Decorating Supply – Specialty supplies for large projects.
  • ReStore (Habitat for Humanity) – Discounted tools and materials.

4. Develop Your Design

This is where creativity comes to life. Here’s how to approach it:

  1. Research inspiration—Look at Portland’s existing murals (e.g., the "Portland Streetcar" series) and local themes like forestry, Indigenous culture, or music.
  2. Sketch ideas—Use thumbnails to explore compositions. Think about:
    • Scale—Will the mural be large and bold or detailed and intricate?
    • Color palette—Portland’s murals often use earthy tones, bright accents, or neon colors.
    • Storytelling—Does the mural tell a narrative (e.g., a journey through Portland’s history)?
  3. Create a mockup—Use digital tools like Procreate or Photoshop to refine your design before committing to the wall.
  4. Get feedback—Share your sketches with peers, teachers, or community members to refine the concept.

    5. Example Portland mural themes:

    • "Portland’s Wild Places"—Featuring Mount Hood, forests, and wildlife.
    • "Voices of Portland"—Portraits of local activists, musicians, or historical figures.
    • "Futuristic Portland"—A sci-fi take on the city’s skyline and tech scene.

    5. Prepare the Surface

    Even the best design will fail if the wall isn’t properly prepped. Follow these steps:

    1. Clean the surface—Remove dirt, peeling paint, or debris with a wire brush or pressure washer.
    2. Fill cracks and holes—Use spackle or joint compound, then sand smooth.
    3. Apply primer—This creates a smooth, even base for paint. Gesso works well for outdoor murals.
    4. Tape off edges—Use painter’s tape to protect surrounding areas (e.g., windows, sidewalks).

    Pro tip: If the mural is on brick or stucco, you may need to etch the surface with muratic acid (follow safety guidelines carefully).

    6. Transfer Your Design to the Wall

    Now it’s time to bring your sketch to life. Common methods include:

    • Tracing paper—Place your design over the wall and trace with a pencil.
    • Projector method—Use a projector to enlarge your sketch onto the wall (great for large designs).
    • Stencils—Cut shapes from cardboard or thin plastic for precise details.
    • Grid method—Divide your design into a grid and transfer it to the wall section by section.

    Common mistakes to avoid:

    • Skipping the pencil sketch—Always outline your design first to ensure proportions are correct.
    • Using cheap tracing paper—It can tear easily. Opt for vellum or heavy-duty tracing paper.
    • Ignoring weather conditions—If painting outdoors, avoid rain or extreme heat.

    7. Paint the Mural

    This is where the magic happens. Here’s how to approach it:

    1. Start with the background—Use a roller for large areas, then add details with brushes.
    2. Work in layers—Let each layer dry before adding the next to avoid smudging.
    3. Use the right tools—
      • Brushes—Flat brushes for broad strokes, detail brushes for fine lines.
      • Spray paint—Only if permitted (check local regulations).
      • Airbrush—For smooth gradients (requires practice).
    4. Blend colors seamlessly—Use a damp sponge or brush to soften edges.
    5. Add texture (optional)—For a 3D effect, use plaster or sand while paint is wet.

    Portland-specific painting tips:

    • Use acrylic paint—It’s durable, weather-resistant, and easy to clean.
    • Test colors first—Portland’s overcast weather can make colors appear different in sunlight.
    • Consider UV-resistant paint—Prevents fading from sun exposure.

    8. Seal and Protect the Mural

    Once the mural is dry, seal it to:

    • Protect against rain, wind, and UV rays.
    • Extend its lifespan (outdoor murals last 5–10 years without sealing).
    • Make it easier to clean (sealed murals resist dirt and grime).

    Sealing options:

    • Acrylic sealer—Affordable and easy to apply (spray or brush-on).
    • Polyurethane sealer—More durable but can yellow over time.
    • Wax finish—Gives a glossy look but requires more maintenance.

    Application steps:

    1. Clean the mural gently with a damp cloth.
    2. Apply sealer in thin, even coats (follow product instructions).
    3. Let each coat dry completely before adding another.
    4. Avoid sealing in direct sunlight or high humidity.
